# Spec RL-15: Overlap policies and a debrief SLA

## Status
Approved after review round 2 (`reviews/RL-15/r2-approved.md`). Revision 3 applies its suggestions
F14–F17; revision 2 answered round 1. Revision 4 (during implementation, checked by the diff review):
AC-04's trial goal is fired after slot 1 (one fired before slot 1 is superseded by slot 1, as the
contract says), so `superseded` is `[T, G1]`; AC-09's later change of F is a fold that resolves it;
the carry cap keeps the hub's item order (RL-2) (most severe, then first seen, then fp).

Source: [harness efficiency and insights](../analysis/2026-11-19-harness-efficiency-and-insights.md),
item I-5 (debrief SLA and overlap policy) as amended by §6 (C-1: the open goals that silenced pulses
were trial goals; `supersede` is mainly for them). Builds on RL-2 (review records, fold, items,
lineages), RL-3 (pulses, slots, `overlap`, `maxGoalAge`), RL-4 (debriefs), RL-9 (sinks, clean-audit
goals), RL-11 (trial fires, `/healthz` features) and RL-12 (stage times, run cards, the health
scorecard). Release 0.1.37.

## Problem

What the code does today (read for this spec):

- `overlap: skip` (the default) skips a slot only while an earlier, unfinished, non-trial goal of the
  pulse has **machine work** (`overlapBusy`, src/core/pulse.ts: a headless step, or the slot's run
  step, that is `ready`, `claimed` or `paused`). Since 0.1.25 a goal that waits only on a person (a
  debrief, any other interactive step) does **not** block, and since 0.1.30 a trial goal never
  blocks. `overlap: allow` never skips. So a debrief no longer silences the schedule by itself.

What is still lost:

1. **A slot that overlaps is gone for a whole period.** A weekly audit whose previous run (or its
   validator) is still busy at the due time is `skipped`; the next run is a week later, two weeks after
   the last. Nothing runs it when the busy goal ends a few minutes later.
2. **Open debriefs pile up.** Each slot creates a goal with its own debrief, so a person who does not
   debrief for three periods has three open goals whose debriefs list mostly the same items, and the
   trial goals of a tuning session stay open next to them (§6 C-1). Nobody can tell from a debrief
   which of them still matters.
3. **Nobody is told that a debrief waits too long**, and low-severity items that an agent validator
   already confirmed wait on a person like the severe ones.

What the fold actually carries (checked against `foldItems`, src/core/review.ts, and `debriefData`,
src/hub/service.ts): the fold's `carried` counts items raised again whose row is `dismissed` (a
dismissal carried forward), **not** open items. An open item of an earlier goal that the next audit
raises again is a `repeat` and keeps its state (`open`, `confirmed`), so the next debrief lists it. One
the next audit does not raise again is `resolved` when the next record has `coverage: complete` (its
fold base is the earlier goal's complete record), and **stays open but in no debrief** when the record
is partial, because a debrief lists only the items of its own run's record. So "the next debrief covers
both runs" needs a carry that this spec adds (below).

## Scope

In:
- `PulseCreate.overlap` and `PulseUpdate.overlap` take `queue` and `supersede` besides `skip` and
  `allow`; the default stays `skip`.
- `queue`: a slot that `skip` would skip waits (slot state `queued`) and fires once no goal blocks; at
  most one queued slot per pulse.
- `supersede`: a fire that creates a goal first drops the earlier goals of the pulse that only wait on
  people (nobody holds a step), sinks what they filed, and carries their undecided debrief items into
  the new goal's debrief.
- A debrief SLA: preset-level `debrief: {sla, auto_accept}` (copied onto each debrief step's policy) or
  the step fields `sla` and `auto_accept`. Past the SLA the hub raises an attention item for the
  assignee, past twice the SLA also for the pulse owner (the goal owner for a goal without a pulse),
  and emits events.
- Opt-in **auto-accept** after the SLA (I-5's "auto-file"): items in a `ready` debrief that an agent
  confirmed, at a severity listed (S3, S4 only), are set `accepted` by the hub, recorded with a
  non-person decider, revertible like a debrief decision.
- `GET /v1/health`: slot count `queued`; `blockedBy` also for `queue` and `supersede`; per pulse the
  overdue debriefs (`debriefs.overdue`, `debriefs.breaches`). Run cards: `queued` and `superseded`.
- Two new `/healthz` features: `debrief-sla` and `overlap-policy`.
- CLI: `mesh pulse add|update --overlap skip|allow|queue|supersede`; `mesh pulse fire` prints a queued
  slot.

Out:
- Closing a debrief the hub emptied by auto-accept (the assignee closes it, or `supersede` drops its
  goal at the next slot).
- Setting a `link` or sinking on auto-accept: the hub cannot create a tracker entry, so an
  auto-accepted item is `accepted` without a link (not "filed" in the RL-9 sense); a later filer stage
  that consumes `accepted` files it.
- Messages to sessions (`queueMessage`) for an overdue debrief: the attention item and the events are
  the signal.
- A limit on how long a queued slot waits other than the rules below; a per-pulse SLA; SLA for steps
  that are not debriefs.
- Web and TUI changes beyond what renders slot states generically today (both print `state` as is).

## Interface contracts

### Common names

- **Open goal**: a goal not `done` and not `dropped`.
- **Busy goal** of a pulse at a fire: an open goal named by a `fired` slot of the pulse (other than the
  slot being fired), not a trial goal, for which `overlapBusy(steps, runStepOf(goal))` names a step.
  This is exactly what `skip` checks today; the first such goal in slot order (`at` desc, `slot` desc)
  is **the running goal**.
- **Waiting goal** of a pulse at a non-trial fire: an open goal named by a `fired` slot of the pulse
  (other than the slot being fired), trial goals **included**, for which `overlapBusy(steps,
  runStepOf(goal))` names no step and no step is `claimed` or `paused` (the rule `mesh goal drop`
  applies, service `dropGoalByPerson`).
- **Debrief step**, **assignee**, **debrief items**, **revertable** as RL-4; the stage times
  `readyAt`, `cycleReadyAt` as RL-12. A debrief's **waiting since** = `cycleReadyAt ?? readyAt`
  (RL-12, computed at the time of the check); absent when both are.
- **SLA debrief**: a debrief step with `policy.sla` whose goal is open and **not a trial goal** (trial
  goals never raise SLA events, attention items or auto-accepts: a trial debrief nobody meant to do is
  the §6 C-1 case, and its auto-accepts would change the production lineage's items).
- **Current round** of a step = its number of rounds + 1.

### Presets (src/core/presets.ts, src/core/policy.ts)

- A preset's YAML may have a top-level `debrief:` mapping, strict: `{ sla?: DurationSpec, auto_accept?:
  ("S3"|"S4")[] }`. `parsePreset` validates it (zod messages as for the step fields) and keeps it as
  `Preset.debrief`; `presetText` writes it back. A preset without `debrief:` parses and renders exactly
  as today.
- `instantiate` copies `debrief.sla` and `debrief.auto_accept` onto every step with `mode: debrief`
  that does not set that field itself (a step's own value wins). Steps of other modes are unchanged.
- `validatePolicies` adds, exactly:
  - `step "<id>": sla needs mode: debrief` (a non-debrief step with `sla`);
  - `step "<id>": auto_accept needs mode: debrief` (a non-debrief step with `auto_accept`);
  - `step "<id>": auto_accept needs sla`;
  - `step "<id>": auto_accept needs accepted in may_set`;
  - `step "<id>": auto_accept needs confirmed in consumes` (the debrief default `consumes` counts).
- `sla` range: 1h to 30d → else `sla is 1h to 30d` (zod refinement, path `sla`).
- `policyOf` copies `sla` and `auto_accept` (so a goal's steps carry them).

### Overlap at a fire (`fireSlot`, src/hub/service.ts)

Order inside the fire's transaction (unchanged first steps): insert the slot row; retire pending slots
whose window is over; abandon started goals past `maxGoalAge`, except that under `supersede` a
**non-trial** fire does not abandon a waiting goal (it is superseded below, with carry and sink, when
the fire creates a goal; when the fire is skipped it stays for a later fire). Every other goal
past `maxGoalAge` (busy, or with a held step) is abandoned under every policy as today. Then:

| `overlap` | A busy goal exists | No busy goal |
|---|---|---|
| `allow` | create the goal (today) | create the goal (today) |
| `skip` | slot `skipped`, reason `overlap`, `running` (today) | create the goal (today) |
| `queue` | **queue** (below); a trial fire is `skipped` as with `skip` | replace the queued slot if any (below), then create the goal |
| `supersede` | slot `skipped`, reason `overlap`, `running` (as `skip`) | a non-trial fire **supersedes** (below), then creates the goal; a trial fire creates the goal without superseding |

A trial fire never queues, never replaces a queued slot and never supersedes: under `queue` and
`supersede` it behaves exactly as under `skip`.

**Queue.** The slot is saved `state: "queued"`, `queued: true`, `running` = the running goal, no
`goal`, `decidedAt` = now; event `pulse.queued {pulse, slot, running}` (`user` for a manual fire). If
another slot of the pulse is `queued` already, that one is first **replaced**: saved `state:
"skipped"`, `reason: "overlap"`, `decidedAt` = now (keeping its `running` and `queued: true`), event
`pulse.skipped {pulse, slot, reason: "overlap", running, replacedBy: <the new slot>}`. So at most one
slot of a pulse is `queued`, always the newest. A non-trial fire that creates a goal also replaces a queued slot
(same event, `replacedBy` the slot that fired).

**Release** (each hub tick, after `advancePending` and before `fireDue`, one `goalTx` per queued
slot): for each
`queued` slot (store `listSlots({state: "queued"})`):
- pulse deleted, or its `overlap` is no longer `queue` → the slot is replaced as above with
  `replacedBy` absent (event `pulse.skipped {pulse, slot, reason: "overlap", running}`);
- the pulse owner is no longer a member (the `firePulseTx` test) → nothing (the next due fire pauses
  the pulse);
- pulse paused → nothing (it waits; a fire after resume replaces it);
- a busy goal exists (the busy test of a fire, without retire or abandon; health `blockedBy` may
  show nothing while a goal the next fire would abandon still holds the slot) → nothing;
- else the slot **fires**: the goal is created as by a fire (same title, refs, comment, trial never),
  the slot is saved `state: "fired"`, `goal`, `firedAt` = now, `until` = now + `windowMs`,
  `decidedAt` = now, `queued: true` kept; event `pulse.fired {pulse, slot, queued: true}` (plus `manual: true` for a manual slot;
  no `user`);
  then one attempt as at a fire. `nextAt` is not changed.

A manual non-trial fire queues like a scheduled one. `POST /v1/pulses/:id/fire` replies 201 with the
`queued` slot.

**Supersede.** Before the new goal is created, for each waiting goal `G` of the pulse, in slot order:
1. **Carry.** For each debrief step `D` of `G` not `done`, `skipped` or `dropped`: the fps of `D`'s
   debrief items now (its run's record items **and** its own carry, below) whose row state is in `D`'s
   `consumes` → a `Carry {step: local(D), lineage: lineage of D's run, fps}`; merged into the new slot's
   `carry` by `(step, lineage)` (union, sorted; at most 1000 fps per entry, kept in the hub's item order (RL-2): most severe first, then
   first seen, then `fp`, when there are more). Entries with no fps are left out.
2. **Sink.** For each step `S` of `G` with `policy.sink` that the drop below will drop (not `done`,
   `skipped`, `dropped` or `failed`): the RL-9 sink
   of `S` runs as at `S`'s close (`runSink`: the items filed in `G` go to their clean-audit goals; a
   sink failure is noted, not thrown).
3. **Drop.** `G` is dropped as a pulse drops a goal (`dropGoal`), reason `superseded`: every step not
   `done`, `skipped`, `dropped` or `failed` is `dropped`, open offers withdrawn; events `goal.dropped
   {reason: "superseded"}` and `step.dropped {reason: "superseded"}`. `G`'s slot keeps its state.
4. Event `pulse.superseded {pulse, slot: <new slot>, goal: G, from: <G's slot>}`.

The new slot is saved with `superseded` = the dropped goal ids (when any) and `carry` (when any).
Items, dispositions, review records and finding steps of `G` are not changed by the drop (RL-9:
clean-audit goals are never pulse goals, so they are never superseded).

**Carry into the new debrief.** A debrief step `D` of a goal `N` whose `refs.slot` names a slot with
`carry`: its **debrief set** = the fps of its run's latest record (none when the run has no record) ∪ the fps of every `carry` entry with
`step` = `local(D)` and `lineage` = the lineage of `D`'s run. Everywhere RL-4 uses "the items of the
run's record" for a debrief, it uses the debrief set: the debrief items (`GET /v1/steps/:id/debrief`,
`GET /v1/debriefs` counts, the `debrief items` context input), the `when` check of the debrief
(`holdsFor`), `POST /v1/steps/:id/dispositions` (`no item <fp> in the record of <run>` only when the fp
is outside the set) and the debrief's own review record (same check). A carried item's `evidence` (in
`DebriefItem`) is that of the first item with its fp in the review record named by the row's `last`
(`goal`, `step`, `round`); `[]` when that record has none. A carried fp is listed whenever its row state is in `D`'s `consumes`, whatever the coverage of the
new record (a carried item dismissed or accepted meanwhile drops out). A debrief without carry
behaves as today. Carried items reach the debrief without the new goal's validator seeing them
(carry is for debriefs only); the guide says so. A carry is keyed by the debrief's local id and its
run's lineage: when a pulse update changes the preset's debrief id or the audit's agent between the
two goals, the carry matches nothing (the items stay open on their lineage); the guide says so.
Steps that are not debriefs never use carry.

### Debrief SLA (tick)

For a debrief `D` with `policy.sla` (`slaMs` = its `sla` in milliseconds) in an open goal, in state
`ready`, `claimed` or `paused`, with a waiting since `t` (trial goals included here; only the tick and
attention below restrict themselves to SLA debriefs):
- **due** = `t + slaMs`; **breached** when `now ≥ due`; **escalated** when `now ≥ t + 2 × slaMs`.
- **Owner** (escalation user): when the goal has `refs.pulse` and that pulse exists (deleted or not),
  the pulse's `owner`; else the goal's `ownerId`.

Each hub tick (after the pause and offer checks, before `advancePending`), in one `goalTx` per step, for each
SLA debrief `D` (store: `listSteps({state})` for the three states, kept when `mode` is `debrief` and `sla` is
set):
1. When `D.overdue?.since` ≠ `t`, the cycle is new: `overdue` := `{since: t}`.
2. Breached and no `assigneeAt` → `assigneeAt` := now; event `debrief.overdue {to: "assignee", user:
   <assignee>, round, since: t, dueAt: due, slaMs}` (goal, step).
3. Escalated, no `ownerAt` and the owner ≠ the assignee → `ownerAt` := now; event `debrief.overdue
   {to: "owner", user: <owner>, round, since: t, dueAt: t + 2 × slaMs, slaMs}`.
4. Breached, `D` is `ready`, `policy.auto_accept` set → **auto-accept** (below); `autoAt` := now when
   it applied at least one item.
5. The step is saved only when `overdue` changed, with `updatedAt` unchanged (the record is the hub's
   bookkeeping, not a step change).

`round` = `D`'s current round. A debrief without `sla`, or whose waiting since is absent, is never
overdue.

**Auto-accept.** Over `D`'s debrief items (debrief set, `consumes` filter, RL-4 order), an item is
**eligible** when its row is `confirmed`, its `severity` is in `auto_accept`, the latest
`kind: "disposition"` entry of its history is `confirmed` with `decider.kind` `agent`, and its history
has no disposition entry with `via: "auto-accept"` and `step` = `D.id` (an item whose auto-accept was
reverted is never auto-accepted again by `D`). For each eligible item, in that order, the hub applies
RL-2 "Applying a disposition" with:
- `disposition: "accepted"`, no `link`, `until`, `requires` or `answer`;
- `decider` = `SLA_DECIDER` (`{kind: "agent", key: "mesh/debrief-sla"}`), `via: "auto-accept"`;
- `reason` = `accepted after the debrief SLA (<sla>): confirmed by <key of the confirming entry's decider> at <severity>`;
- `goal`, `step` = `D`'s, `round` = `D`'s current round, `at` = now;

then adds a note on `D` (user = the goal owner) `auto-accept: accepted <fp> "<title>": <reason>`
(event `step.noted`) and emits `item.disposed {lineage, fp, disposition: "accepted", from:
"confirmed", decider: "agent", via: "auto-accept", round}` (no `user`). After the items, when at least
one was applied: event `debrief.auto_accepted {round, items: <n>}` (goal, step). `syncDisposition`
runs as for any disposition (it acts on dismissals and deferrals only, so nothing changes). S1 and S2
items are never eligible.

**Revert.** `POST /v1/steps/:id/dispositions/revert` treats a `via: "auto-accept"` entry like a
`debrief-session` one: the latest disposition of the fp by that step with either via is the one
reverted; it is revertable under the RL-4 rule (the latest history entry of the item); RL-4's
permission (the decider user, the goal owner or an admin) is extended by **the debrief's assignee**
(for both vias); an auto-accept has no decider user. The 403 text becomes `only <names>, the goal
owner or an admin can revert this`, `<names>` = the decider user (person deciders only) and the
assignee, de-duplicated, joined with `, ` (a debrief-session decision by its assignee reads as
today). The revert entry, note, `item.reverted` event and
`syncRevert` are as RL-4. `GET /v1/steps/:id/debrief` `decided` lists the step's dispositions with
`via` `debrief-session` **or** `auto-accept` (insertion order, each with `revertable`).
`GET /v1/debriefs` `decided` is unchanged (the caller's own decisions).

**The debrief's own decisions.** An auto-accept row of `D` in the current round counts as a session
decision of its fp: RL-4's `sessionDecision` (the debrief's record: same disposition → `already`,
another → the existing 422 text) and `disposeStep`'s change-of-mind rule (`own`) take rows with `via`
`debrief-session` **or** `auto-accept` of this step and round. So a person may overrule an
auto-accept in the session (`mesh_dispose` with another disposition) without a revert.

**Attention.** `GET /v1/attention` (and the `attention` of `GET /v1/live`) gains, for each breached
SLA debrief computed at the read (not from `overdue`): an item `{kind: "debrief_overdue", text: "Debrief
<step> \"<title>\" is overdue: waiting since <ISO of since>, SLA <sla>", goal, step, at: dueAt}` for
the assignee once breached, and for the owner once escalated (one item per viewer and step).

### `GET /v1/health` (RL-12, changed)

- `slots` (pulse and total) gains `queued`: how many of the pulse's scheduled slots in the window are
  in state `queued` now. `due` still counts every scheduled slot in the window.
- `blockedBy`: computed as RL-12 when `overlap` is `skip`, `queue` or `supersede`; absent with
  `allow`.
- `debriefs` (pulse) gains `overdue` (the number of its open debriefs, as RL-12 counts `open`, that are
  breached now) and `breaches`: one `DebriefBreach` per such debrief, ordered by `breachedAt`, then
  `step`: `assignee` resolved now, `owner` as above, `slaMs`, `since` (waiting since), `breachedAt` =
  `since + slaMs`, `escalated`, `autoAccepted` = the number of its `via: "auto-accept"` dispositions in
  its current round. A trial goal's debrief counts only with `trial=include` (as RL-12 `open`).
- `total.debriefs.overdue` = the sum over the listed pulses.

### Run cards (`GET /v1/pulses/:id/runs`, changed)

`queued` = the slot has the mark; `superseded` = the slot's list or `[]`. A `queued` slot's card has no
goal (`stages: []`, zeros). `startWaitMs` of a slot with `queued: true` is `startedAt − firedAt` (as a
manual or catch-up slot). Nothing else changes.

### CLI (src/cli/pulse-commands.ts)

- `--overlap` takes `skip|allow|queue|supersede` (usage text updated); another value is refused by the
  hub's 400 as today.
- A `skipped` reply prints `(overlap: <the pulse's overlap>)`, read with `GET /v1/pulses/:id` (today's
  text when the policy is `skip`).
- `mesh pulse fire <id>` with a `queued` reply prints `Pulse <id> queued slot <slot>: <running> is
  still running (overlap: queue); it fires when that ends.` and exits 0.

## Compatibility

- **No migration** (schema 11): slot state is a text column; every new field lives in the JSON
  `value` of pulses, slots, steps and item history.
- Existing pulses (`skip`, `allow`), presets (no `debrief:`) and steps (no `sla`) parse and behave
  unchanged; `PulseCreate` without `overlap` is `skip`.
- A client detects the policies by `overlap-policy` and the SLA by `debrief-sla` in `/healthz`
  `features`.
- Enum widenings (`overlap`, slot `state`, `via`, attention `kind`, `goal.dropped.reason`) are visible
  to clients that list them exhaustively; the guide says so.
